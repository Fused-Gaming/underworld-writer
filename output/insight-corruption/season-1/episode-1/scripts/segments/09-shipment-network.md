# 09 — How the Shipment Network Worked — Part 1

**Segment ID:** shipment-network
**Type:** case-specific
**Words:** 165
**Estimated runtime:** 1:08
**Claim IDs:** C01, C02, C05, C06

The federal investigation described a long shipping history.

According to the U.S. Attorney’s Office, the original complaint alleged that between October 2015 and January 2023 at least sixty-one shipments were mailed to Segovia’s home from overseas locations including Hong Kong, Hungary, India, and Singapore. Customs descriptions presented the packages as ordinary consumer goods. The examples publicly identified included descriptions such as wedding-related items, makeup, food, and sweets.

That kind of labeling was significant because the contents did not always match the manifests. Between July 2019 and January 2023, officials intercepted five shipments and found thousands of controlled-substance pills, including tramadol and tapentadol. Some parcels were described by federal authorities as containing drugs worth thousands of dollars.

The complaint also described encrypted WhatsApp communications. Investigators alleged that over a period from January 2020 into March 2023, Segovia exchanged hundreds of messages with a person using a telephone number associated with India. Those messages, the government said, were used to coordinate receiving and sending pill shipments.
