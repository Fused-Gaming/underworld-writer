## Editorial thesis

This episode is about an oversight question, not a pre-decided criminal conclusion: what due diligence and accountability controls should exist when publicly funded housing assistance intersects with a landlord or property that already appears in a meaningful municipal enforcement/integrity record?

The episode deliberately separates two evidence layers:

1. **Adjudicated Oakland PEC findings (2015–2016 conduct, 2021 hearing decision)** — Thomas Espinosa was found to have committed 43 Oakland ethics-law violations. The findings state that he solicited/received four $300 payments from Elizabeth Williams in exchange for permit/inspection-related official acts.
2. **2024–2026 SSVF/Insight Housing dispute** — retained program records, correspondence, court-related materials, public-records efforts, and an OPD report contain allegations about housing conditions, fund handling, eviction, and retaliation. These later allegations are not presented as adjudicated criminal findings.

## Strongest verified facts

- Oakland PEC Case No. 16-14 identifies Thomas Espinosa as an Oakland building inspector and concludes that he committed 43 ethics-law violations involving bribery, misuse of authority, conflicts, misuse of public resources, and disclosure failures.
- The same PEC findings identify Elizabeth Williams as an Oakland landlord and describe four separate $300 solicitations tied to permits/inspections at Oakland properties in 2015–2016.
- The ethics findings are findings against Espinosa. Do **not** describe them as a criminal conviction of Williams.
- Retained SSVF/Insight Housing records show a publicly funded veteran housing placement beginning in December 2024 at 1055 72nd Avenue, Oakland, associated in later records with Williams.
- Retained Insight Housing correspondence memorializes complaints about pest/security/property conditions.
- OPD report T26008570 records allegations made by the reporting party about the housing placement, public funds, eviction, and retaliation. It is proof that the allegations were reported, not proof that they occurred as alleged.

## Source tiers

### Tier 1 — Government adjudication / direct public record
- Oakland Public Ethics Commission, Case No. 16-14, Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law.
- City of Oakland PEC meeting materials containing the hearing officer recommendation and exhibits.

### Tier 1/2 — Direct records retained by reporting party
- SSVF approval/support records.
- Insight Housing correspondence reflecting reported habitability/security concerns.
- Court filings/orders where directly obtained.
- Public-records responses and agency correspondence.

### Tier 2 — Police report as a record of allegations
- Oakland Police Department report T26008570 / original report T26002522.
- Safe use: “The report records/alleges…”
- Unsafe use: “The named subjects committed fraud/theft/conspiracy.”

### Tier 3 — Referenced but not yet independently matched
- ACCELA Record #2600830 and historical code-enforcement records.
- March 2019 violation notices.
- Inspector Marlette notes.
- Fire inspection records identified in the police-report narrative.
- Complete SSVF disbursement ledger and recipient/payment chain.

## Right-of-reply targets

### Elizabeth Williams / EAW Rentals
Questions:
1. What is your ownership/management relationship to 1055 72nd Avenue?
2. Did you receive rent, deposit, or other funds connected to the SSVF placement? If so, how much and on what dates?
3. What inspections occurred before and during the placement?
4. Were there outstanding code/fire issues at the property before placement?
5. What is your response to the participant's allegations concerning property conditions and the later eviction?
6. Is there context you believe the PEC record omits or that is important to understanding your role in the 2015–2016 transactions?

### Insight Housing
Questions:
1. What inspection and landlord-screening process was used for this placement?
2. Were Oakland code-enforcement/fire records checked before approval?
3. What public funds were authorized, paid, and to whom?
4. What complaints were received from the participant, and what corrective actions followed?
5. Were promised inspections/home visits/conflict-resolution services performed?
6. What internal appeal or independent complaint process was available?
7. What is Insight Housing's response to allegations in OPD report T26008570?

### VA / SSVF oversight
Questions:
1. What monitoring requirements apply to grantees/subgrantees making placements?
2. What records exist for inspection, eligibility, payment approval, and complaint handling in this case?
3. What review occurred after the participant escalated complaints?

## Studio risk notes

- Avoid dramatic delivery on named-person allegations.
- Use “the PEC found” only for the Espinosa ethics case.
- Use “the reporting party alleges,” “the police report records,” or “retained correspondence shows” for later events depending on source.
- Do not say the old PEC case proves later SSVF fraud.
- Do not imply Insight Housing participated in the 2015–2016 permit transactions.
- Do not state the amount of later public-money loss as a verified theft amount unless the underlying payment ledger is obtained.
