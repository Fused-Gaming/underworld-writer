## Editorial thesis

This episode is about an oversight question, not a pre-decided criminal conclusion: what due diligence and accountability controls should exist when publicly funded housing assistance intersects with a landlord or property that already appears in a meaningful municipal enforcement/integrity record?

The episode deliberately separates two evidence layers:

1. **Adjudicated Oakland PEC findings (2015–2016 conduct, 2021 hearing decision)** — Thomas Espinosa was found to have committed 43 Oakland ethics-law violations. The findings state that he solicited/received four $300 payments from Elizabeth Williams in exchange for permit/inspection-related official acts.
2. **2024–2026 SSVF/Insight Housing dispute** — retained program records, correspondence, court-related materials, public-records efforts, and an OPD report contain allegations about housing conditions, fund handling, eviction, and retaliation. These later allegations are not presented as adjudicated criminal findings.

## Strongest verified facts

- Oakland PEC Case No. 16-14 identifies Thomas Espinosa as an Oakland building inspector and concludes that he committed 43 ethics-law violations involving bribery, misuse of authority, conflicts, misuse of public resources, and disclosure failures.
- The same PEC findings identify Elizabeth Williams as an Oakland landlord and describe four separate $300 solicitations tied to permits/inspections at Oakland properties in 2015–2016.
- The ethics findings are findings against Espinosa. Do **not** describe them as a criminal conviction of Williams.
- Retained SSVF/Insight Housing records show a publicly funded veteran housing placement beginning in December 2024 at 1055 72nd Avenue, Oakland, associated in later records with Williams.
- Retained Insight Housing correspondence memorializes complaints about pest/security/property conditions.
- OPD report T26008570 records allegations made by the reporting party about the housing placement, public funds, eviction, and retaliation. It is proof that the allegations were reported, not proof that they occurred as alleged.

## New reporting-layer facts (added after issue #148 follow-up comment, Sept 26 2026)

Press coverage of the *final* Commission decision — separate from the hearing-officer recommendation already cited above — substantially widens the Espinosa/Williams record. Treat these as a second, later-dated primary layer, not a replacement for the hearing-officer findings already scripted:

- **September 17, 2021 — final Commission decision:** the Commission raised Espinosa's fine to **$309,600** (up from the hearing officer's recommended ~$210,000) across what press reports describe as **47** violations — a discrepancy from the "43" in the hearing-officer document we hold; we have not yet obtained the Commission's own final written decision to reconcile the count. Commission chair Michael MacDonald called it the most extensive case the PEC had ever investigated. (Oaklandside, Sept. 28, 2021; CBS News/KPIX.)
- **The contractor conflict:** beyond the four $300 solicitations, Espinosa was also paid by Williams to work as her electrical/general contractor on her rental properties, then inspected his own work and closed at least one code case against her (October 2015) without disclosing the conflict on his Form 700. Reported total payments from Williams to Espinosa are inconsistent across outlets — **$176,000** (Oaklandside) vs. **~$112,000** (East Bay Express) — and neither number is yet matched to the Commission's underlying financial exhibits. Flag as an open reconciliation item, not a settled figure.
- **A second inspector, Anthony Harbaugh:** a PEC hearing officer separately found Harbaugh committed ethics violations (8 per the hearing officer; press reports up to 11), including accepting a $300 payment from Espinosa to approve electrical work at a Williams property after two other inspectors had rejected it, and failing to report income he received from Williams (Jan. 2015–Sept. 2016). Harbaugh was fined $55,000. **Add Harbaugh as a named character** in the episode config — he is currently missing.
- **FBI immunity:** East Bay Express reports Williams received immunity from the FBI to testify about the scheme. Not independently confirmed against a DOJ/FBI record — keep at Tier 2 ("reported to have received immunity") rather than stating it as adjudicated fact.
- **Pattern beyond Williams:** East Bay Express also reports separate Espinosa extortion allegations involving landlord Alexandre Machado ($12,850 demanded to lift a stop-work order) and broker Bill Charman ($1,500 personal check to close a case). Useful as pattern/context, not core to the Williams/Insight Housing thread — a line or two at most, clearly attributed.
- **ACCELA Case 2600830 is now directly confirmed** (property: 1055 72nd Ave, Oakland; parcel 041 414600600; status "OP-Insp-Not Abated"), matching the placement address exactly. It references the Jan. 4, 2026 VA Homeless Veterans Hotline complaint and the Jan. 8, 2026 summons — consistent with the complainant's chronology already in the script. This clears part of the "Tier 3" ACCELA item; the *full* case history/prior violations still need to be pulled.
- **732 Apgar Street fire (2018, per Indybay):** a tenant account alleges an electrical fire displaced three units and that Williams demanded rent despite uninhabitable conditions; a separate tenant (Danielle Johnson, 2731 Market St.) alleged Harbaugh was personally close to Williams and refused a verification inspection. This is single-sourced to one 2018 movement-press item and has **not** been corroborated against OFD incident records or a second outlet — keep at Tier 3, attribute explicitly to "a 2018 Indybay account of tenant complaints," and do not state it as established fact.

## Independent corroboration: "Judge Faith" small-claims arbitration (Season 1, Episode 4)

The YouTube link from issue #148 is an episode of *Judge Faith*, a binding small-claims arbitration TV show, captioned *Johnson v. Williams*. Reviewed via transcript. This is a second, independent primary source — separate from the PEC file, the Indybay article, and the OPD report — involving the same landlord and, notably, the same tenant named in the 2018 Indybay piece:

- **Tier 1 (on-the-record arbitration judgment):** Tenant Danielle Johnson sued landlord Elizabeth Williams over habitability conditions (a worker's leg breaking through a ceiling during renovation, a leaking roof soaking a mattress during a storm, a bathroom ceiling collapsing on more than one occasion, and a City-documented sewage backup at the unit shortly before the hearing). The arbitrator ruled for Johnson and awarded $525 (bed replacement) + $1,000 punitive + $1,000 for breach of the implied warranty of habitability — **$1,525 total** — finding Williams breached that warranty. Williams's toilet-damage countersuit was not awarded.
- **Tier 2 (sworn but unmatched testimony):** Johnson testified on the record that "the City of Oakland sued her for 11 properties in 2006" and that there were "3 articles about her being a slumlord of Oakland." This is Johnson's own testimony, not yet independently matched by us to a specific court filing, but it is consistent with — and corroborates the existence of — the 2006 City lawsuit already reported by Indybay.
- **Identifies the same tenant as the Indybay article:** Danielle Johnson is the same tenant Indybay (Nov. 2018) quoted regarding a different Williams property (2731 Market St.) and inspector Anthony Harbaugh. This episode does not itself name Market Street or Harbaugh — it concerns a separate building Williams "bought in January" — so treat it as a second, independent Johnson-v-Williams dispute, not a retelling of the Market Street incident, unless a shared address is confirmed.

## Studio risk notes

- Avoid dramatic delivery on named-person allegations.
- Use “the PEC found” only for the Espinosa ethics case.
- Use “the reporting party alleges,” “the police report records,” or “retained correspondence shows” for later events depending on source.
- Do not say the old PEC case proves later SSVF fraud.
- Do not imply Insight Housing participated in the 2015–2016 permit transactions.
- Do not state the amount of later public-money loss as a verified theft amount unless the underlying payment ledger is obtained.
